\chapter{Threats to Validity}
\label{ch:threats_validity}

In experimental software engineering applied to Multi-Agent Systems, ensuring the validity of empirical results requires adapting traditional research threats to the context of LLM-driven synthetic environments. This chapter structuralizes the evaluation of these threats across four dimensions: Conclusion, Internal, Construct, and External validity, specifically analyzing how the GeoMAS architecture mitigates or acknowledges them.

\section{Conclusion Validity}
Conclusion validity concerns the ability to distinguish true causal signals from environmental noise. 

GeoMAS systematically eliminates environmental noise through a \textbf{strictly deterministic orchestration layer} (fixed map seeds, mathematically rigid resource calculators, and ordered turn execution). Consequently, the execution environment guarantees 100\% reproducibility. The \textit{only} source of variance is the \textbf{stochastic nature of the LLMs}. 

It is a documented phenomenon in LLM inference that even when setting the sampling temperature to strictly $0.0$ (argmax sampling), outputs can still exhibit minor non-deterministic variations due to GPU floating-point math optimizations and batched matrix multiplications. To isolate this specific stochastic noise, the framework standardizes its experimentation by exclusively utilizing the \textbf{DeepSeek V3.2 model} (standard, non-reasoning version) for all agent cognition. This fixes the model architecture and its latent biases as a constant variable across all runs. Furthermore, to prevent "fishing" for results, the analytical methodology relies on a Jupyter Analysis Notebook that computes predefined metrics uniformly across all runs, guaranteeing a pre-declared signal-to-noise evaluation.

\section{Internal Validity}
Internal validity addresses the rigor with which alternative causal explanations can be ruled out when observing an effect. 

GeoMAS achieves near-perfect internal validity through its \textbf{state forking mechanism}. When testing causal hypotheses (e.g., via XAI injections or scenario triggers), the system forks the simulation exactly at a target turn $T$. Because the baseline and the forked simulation share mathematically identical initial conditions and pre-treatment history up to $T$, traditional threats, such as diverging historical lifepaths or differential maturation, are structurally eliminated. However, it must be acknowledged that while the physical constraints are perfectly parallel, the \textbf{stochastic nature of the LLM inference} means that any socio-political divergence post-$T$ is exclusively attributable to the injected condition \textit{plus} minor non-deterministic cognitive variations. Additionally, the simulation suffers zero mortality bias, as the NationAgents cannot drop out of the study.

\section{Construct Validity}
Construct validity examines whether the framework's operationalizations (metrics and treatments) accurately reflect the theoretical phenomena they intend to measure.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Arbitrary Metric Definitions:} Behavioral alignment is quantified via the Deception and Coherence scores. However, the matrices assigning these scores (e.g., penalizing an Authoritarian state $0.95$ for masking Conquest as Democracy) are manually codified heuristics. This represents a threat to construct validity, as it translates complex psychological and diplomatic concepts into arbitrary numerical weights. To mitigate this, the framework pairs these quantitative, heuristic-driven scores with \textit{qualitative answer audits} (side-by-side prompt inspections) to ensure the numerical score matches the actual semantic reasoning of the agent.
    \item \textbf{Bounded Rationality and Context Engineering Bias:} LLM agents do not possess continuous episodic memory. Their perception is strictly bounded by a \texttt{ContextManager} that dynamically prunes historical events to fit within a structured context window. While GeoMAS avoids arbitrary "forgetting" by using a deterministic \textit{salience score} (prioritizing high-impact events over minor messages), this introduces a structural bias, as the definition of what constitutes a "high-salience" event is a hardcoded heuristic. Furthermore, the foundational choice of \textit{what} state data is included in this context window inherently shapes the agent's attention mechanism. While significant architectural effort was expended to ensure both \texttt{System} and \texttt{Input} prompts remain strictly objective, descriptive, and devoid of tonal nudging, the unavoidable fact remains that filtering the simulation state to fit a token budget engineers the boundaries of the agent's simulated perception.
    \item \textbf{Sequential Execution and Cabinet Blindness:} While the three Ministerial agents (Defense, Economy, Foreign) generate their proposals concurrently (producing a "Cabinet Blindness" where they cannot laterally negotiate within the same turn), the \textit{execution} of these approved actions strictly follows a rigid sequence: Economy $\rightarrow$ Defense $\rightarrow$ Foreign. This artificial temporality means diplomatic actions have perfect information regarding current-turn military movements, but the military cannot retroactively react to unexpected diplomatic shifts until the successive turn.
    \item \textbf{Discrete Action Space:} Real-world geopolitics involves infinite nuance and continuous variables. GeoMAS drastically simplifies this by discretizing intents into explicit Pydantic schemas (e.g., picking from six rigid \texttt{ForeignIntentType} enums) and strictly capping actions (e.g., only one diplomatic action per nation per turn, no complex multi-conditional treaties). This forces the LLMs to "quantize" their complex reasoning into blunt mechanical levers, oversimplifying the construct of international relations.
    \item \textbf{Economic Simplification and Engineered Scarcity:} The global economy in GeoMAS is not a dynamic market with pricing elasticity, but a rigid arithmetic system guided by pure calculators. For instance, food and energy consumption are fixed linear rates per capita, while production yields are hardcoded by procedural terrain (e.g., Mountains strictly dictate high materials and low food) to mathematically engineer a tight $\sim 5\%$ baseline surplus. By defining the economy through fixed heuristics rather than emergent market forces, the framework exerts a structural pressure that biases agents toward mercantilist and zero-sum behaviors, representing a significant threat to the construct validity of "rational economic actors".
\end{itemize}

\section{External Validity}
External validity refers to the extent to which the simulation's findings can be generalized to broader contexts.

GeoMAS does not claim to produce predictive digital twins of real-world historical states. The framework is an abstract, stylized representation of geopolitics defined by simplified territorial and resource mechanics. Therefore, the emergent behaviors observed, such as deterrence failures or the formation of bipolar blocs, generalize only to the \textit{theoretical dynamics of rational state actors under scarcity and imperfect information}, rather than predicting specific real-world international relations.

However, the current experimental design presents two significant threats to external validity that limit the broad generalizability of its findings:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Single LLM Architecture:} All experiments and simulations were conducted exclusively utilizing the \textbf{DeepSeek V3.2} model. No other Large Language Models (e.g., GPT-5.2, Claude 4.6) were tested. Consequently, it is unknown whether the observed emergent behaviors are universal properties of LLM-based multi-agent systems or merely artifacts of DeepSeek V3.2's specific alignment, training data, and latent cognitive biases.
    \item \textbf{Single Map Seed Topology:} The baseline simulation was executed and forked entirely on a \textbf{single, specific} \texttt{map\_seed}. While the procedural generation engine theoretically allows for infinite geographic variations, limiting the study to one map topology means that the observed socio-political equilibria might be overly fitted to that specific distribution of resources, chokepoints, and starting borders. Replicating the baseline across multiple seeds would be required to prove true geographic generalizability.
\end{itemize}
