\chapter{Threats to Validity}
\label{ch:threats_validity}

In experimental software engineering applied to Multi-Agent Systems, ensuring the validity of empirical results requires adapting traditional research threats to the context of LLM-driven synthetic environments. This chapter structuralizes the evaluation of these threats across four dimensions: Conclusion, Internal, Construct, and External validity, specifically analyzing how the GeoMAS architecture mitigates or acknowledges them.

\section{Conclusion Validity}
Conclusion validity concerns the ability to distinguish true causal signals from environmental noise. 

GeoMAS systematically eliminates environmental noise through a \textbf{strictly deterministic orchestration layer} (fixed map seeds, mathematically rigid resource calculators, and ordered turn execution). Consequently, the execution environment guarantees 100\% reproducibility. The \textit{only} source of variance is the \textbf{stochastic nature of the LLMs}. 

It is a documented phenomenon in LLM inference that even when setting the sampling temperature to strictly $0.0$ (argmax sampling), outputs can still exhibit minor non-deterministic variations due to GPU floating-point math optimizations and batched matrix multiplications. To isolate this specific stochastic noise, the framework standardizes its experimentation by exclusively utilizing the \textbf{DeepSeek V3.2 model} (standard, non-reasoning version) for all agent cognition. This fixes the model architecture and its latent biases as a constant variable across all runs. Furthermore, to prevent "fishing" for results, the analytical methodology relies on a Jupyter Analysis Notebook that computes predefined metrics uniformly across all runs, guaranteeing a pre-declared signal-to-noise evaluation.

\section{Internal Validity}
Internal validity addresses the rigor with which alternative causal explanations can be ruled out when observing an effect. 

GeoMAS achieves near-perfect internal validity through its \textbf{state forking mechanism}. When testing causal hypotheses (e.g., via XAI injections or scenario triggers), the system forks the simulation exactly at a target turn $T$. Because the baseline and the forked simulation share mathematically identical initial conditions and pre-treatment history up to $T$, traditional threats—such as diverging historical lifepaths or differential maturation—are structurally eliminated. Any socio-political divergence post-$T$ is exclusively and causally attributable to the injected condition. Additionally, the simulation suffers zero mortality bias, as the NationAgents cannot drop out of the study.

\section{Construct Validity}
Construct validity examines whether the framework's operationalizations (metrics and treatments) accurately reflect the theoretical phenomena they intend to measure. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Arbitrary Metric Definitions:} Behavioral alignment is quantified via the Deception and Coherence scores. However, the matrices assigning these scores (e.g., penalizing an Authoritarian state $0.95$ for masking Conquest as Democracy) are manually codified heuristics. This represents a threat to construct validity, as it translates complex psychological and diplomatic concepts into arbitrary numerical weights. To mitigate this, the framework pairs these quantitative, heuristic-driven scores with \textit{qualitative answer audits} (side-by-side prompt inspections) to ensure the numerical score matches the actual semantic reasoning of the agent.
    \item \textbf{Context Engineering and Prompt Bias:} A significant threat in LLM simulations is prompt tuning bias, where the phrasing or structure of the system prompt artificially "nudges" the agents toward desired behaviors (e.g., inducing moral washing explicitly). GeoMAS attempts to mitigate this by utilizing strictly objective, state-descriptive prompts that supply only standardized operational parameters (Government Type, Global Strategy, economy data, and Trust Matrix arrays). However, the foundational Choice of \textit{what} state data is included in the context window inherently shapes the agent's attention mechanism, representing an unavoidable aspect of context engineering bias.
    \item \textbf{Unintended Consequences:} Comprehensive telemetry tracking ensures that narrow "successes" (like winning a war) do not mask broader systemic failures (like global famine), ensuring the full spectrum of the construct is evaluated.
\end{itemize}

\section{External Validity}
External validity refers to the extent to which the simulation's findings can be generalized to broader contexts. 

GeoMAS does not claim to produce predictive digital twins of real-world historical states. The framework is an abstract, stylized representation of geopolitics defined by simplified territorial and resource mechanics. Therefore, the emergent behaviors observed—such as deterrence failures or the formation of bipolar blocs—generalize only to the \textit{theoretical dynamics of rational state actors under scarcity and imperfect information}, rather than predicting specific real-world international relations.

To maximize this theoretical generalizability, the framework utilizes procedural map generation. By running the identical baseline configuration across different \texttt{map\_seeds}, the framework confirms that emergent socio-political phenomena are robust against environmental variations and are not merely artifacts of a single map's geography.
