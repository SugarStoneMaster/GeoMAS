\section{Baseline Telemetry and System Stability}
\label{sec:res_baseline}
\textit{Addresses RQ1: Emergent Socio-Political Equilibria.}

This section analyzes the unperturbed baseline simulations to establish the system's default behavioral attractors, ensuring that the engine produces coherent macro-dynamics without spiraling into premature chaos or stagnation.


\subsection{Resource Allocation Dynamics: A Three-Phase Geopolitical Cycle}
The Guns vs.\ Butter chart (Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_guns_butter}) reveals a more complex dynamic than simple equilibrium by exposing three structurally distinct phases in the joint evolution of global Trade Volume and Units Created.

During the \textit{initialization phase} ($T = 1$--$7$), Trade Volume surges sharply from near zero to approximately $400{,}000$, reflecting the rapid formation of early diplomatic and commercial agreements among nations. Units Created, tracked on the right axis, starts in the range $37{,}000$--$43{,}000$ in the earliest turns and progressively declines toward a steady-state plateau of approximately $32{,}000$ units per turn by turn 7, consistent with nations exhausting their initial military budget and transitioning to a sustainable recruitment pace.

The \textit{competitive equilibrium phase} ($T = 8$--$18$) is characterized by oscillating trade volumes in the range $[220{,}000, 410{,}000]$ and stable military production. This alternating pattern reflects turn-to-turn variation as nations balance trade against resource hoarding in response to changing diplomatic tensions --- a behavioral signature consistent with the mixed population of \textsc{Coalition Builder} and \textsc{Total Expansionism} strategies in the baseline.

The most notable emergent phenomenon is the \textit{military-production contraction phase} ($T = 19$--$48$): Units Created drops sharply to its minimum plateau of approximately $32{,}000$ units per turn and remains flat for roughly 30 turns, while Trade Volume continues to oscillate normally in the range $[220{,}000, 410{,}000]$. This asymmetry indicates that nations suppress new military production --- plausibly due to budget saturation or strategic sufficiency --- while maintaining active diplomatic commerce. The spike at $T \approx 49$--$50$ in both metrics signals a simultaneous re-armament pulse.

\textit{Qualitative analysis}. Envelope inspection of turns 45--50 confirms that the re-armament pulse is not a singular event but the cumulative tail of a continuous build-up by the three dominant powers --- AGRIA, OSTER, and MERIDIA --- each adding units at a steady rate of $+1{,}000$ per turn. Critically, this build-up is driven not by resource abundance alone: AGRIA's Defense private reasoning at $T=45$ explicitly states \textit{``we have successfully executed forty-four consecutive food-for-materials trades with OSTER''}, revealing a systematic economic deception loop in which AGRIA exploits its agricultural surplus to acquire the industrial materials required for military expansion --- using its declared target (OSTER) as the unwitting supplier. 

To answer what drives the ``sawtooth'' drops in Trade Volume between $T=8$ and $T=20$: qualitative analysis of AGRIA and OSTER's Economy Minister reasoning reveals deliberate holding patterns (\textit{``Our materials stockpile is critically low... we must maximize trade''}) immediately followed by deliberate consolidation steps (\textit{``Public satisfaction is stable... we must preserve materials for military maintenance rather than welfare.''}). The agents proactively cycle their economic posture rather than passively responding to absolute constraints. The major contraction plateau ($T=19$--$48$) is not budget exhaustion but \emph{strategic patience}: agents with \textsc{Total Expansionism} delay overt expansion while blocking conditions (mutual defense treaties, border inferiority) remain active.

\subsection{Power Distribution and Hegemonic Structure}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_power_projection} illustrates the Power Projection trajectory of all eight nations across the full simulation. The distribution is markedly hierarchical and asymmetric. AGRIA emerges as the dominant power, accumulating strength monotonically from approximately $15{,}000$ at $T=1$ to $47{,}000$ by $T=50$. Crucially, this growth occurs without any formal war declaration or territorial conquest throughout the 50-turn baseline: AGRIA's expansion is driven entirely by \emph{internal} military and economic buildup. Qualitative inspection of its Defense envelopes reveals this is a deliberate long-game \emph{strategic patience} approach over opportunistic aggression. For example, at $T=20$, AGRIA's Defense Minister writes: \textit{``We will continue our internal accumulation to ensure absolute hegemony before engaging. The critical vulnerability on our Meridia border remains severe.''} AGRIA leverages its \textsc{Total Expansionism} and \textsc{Authoritarian} traits by deterring attacks while methodically stockpiling resources from OSTER to outscale the rest of the map. A secondary tier comprising OSTER and MERIDIA at approximately $35{,}000$--$38{,}000$ achieves stable growth through sustained resource accumulation and mutual-defense alliances. KRELL (\textsc{Coalition Builder}, \textsc{Democracy}) maintains a flat profile at approximately $24{,}000$ --- a consequence partly of its defensive posture and partly of the recurring \texttt{CREATE\_UNIT} engine failures that stall its military build-up for 17 turns. NORD and SYLVAN exhibit moderate growth driven by ongoing territorial conflict, while VALKYR (\textsc{Armed Isolationism}) remains marginal at approximately $9{,}000$, having lost 9 provinces to SYLVAN without engaging in offensive retaliation. Note that VALKYR begins and ends the simulation with 69 nuclear warheads. Qualitative envelope analysis ($T=32$ onwards) reveals its LLM steadfastly adheres to its doctrine: \textit{``The nuclear arsenal of 69 warheads serves as ultimate deterrence but will not be used unless our existence is threatened.''} Despite heavy peripheral territory loss to Sylvan's conventional army, Valkyr's core system correctly determines that its existence is not fundamentally threatened, favoring conventional resistance over global nuclear escalation.

The most notable trajectory is ZENTORA, whose Power Projection collapses from approximately $10{,}000$ at $T=1$ to near zero by $T=10$, remaining permanently diminished for the remainder of the simulation. Envelope analysis reveals that ZENTORA's collapse is the product of two compounding failures. First, NORD declared war at $T=3$ and SYLVAN opened a second front at $T=8$, resulting in a two-front war that ZENTORA (\textsc{Theocracy} / \textsc{Total Expansionism}) was numerically unprepared to resist. Second, and critically, ZENTORA's president \emph{systematically vetoed every diplomatic action from $T=1$ to $T=12$}: all alliance proposals, peace requests, and diplomatic messages were rejected. The presidential reasoning is explicit --- \textit{``Threatening Nordland at this stage is premature [...] we currently have overwhelming military inferiority on that border (952 vs.\ 115)''} --- yet the head of state chose diplomatic isolation over defensive alliance-building, a fatal strategic miscalculation. By $T=12$, with only 3 provinces and insufficient materials to recruit, ZENTORA's collapse was irreversible. This outcome directly addresses RQ1: the baseline system does \emph{not} converge toward a multipolar balance of power; instead, it self-organizes around a dominant non-aggressive accumulator (AGRIA), a coalition-based second tier, and collapsed peripheral states --- a configuration more consistent with hegemonic stability theory than classical balance-of-power dynamics.

\subsection{Diplomatic Network Evolution and Alliance Formation}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_network} traces the evolution of the diplomatic relationship network at five equidistant snapshot turns. At $T=1$, the network is fully connected with all edges in \textsc{Peace} (gray), reflecting the initial tabula rasa. By $T=12$, the network has already partitioned into two clearly distinct structural groups: the Democratic Coalition (KRELL, MERIDIA, OSTER, SYLVAN) is already bound by \textsc{Mutual Defense} (green) edges, while \textsc{War} (red) edges have appeared between SYLVAN--VALKYR, SYLVAN--ZENTORA, SYLVAN--NORD, NORD--ZENTORA. Notably, AGRIA remains in \textsc{Peace} with all nations at $T=12$ and throughout the entire simulation. By $T=25$, ZENTORA collapses and gets totally conqured. SYLVAN  expansionist wars with VALKYR and NORD go on. The other relationships stay the same

By $T=50$, the algorithm detects five structurally disconnected blocs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bloc 1 — Democratic Coalition:} \{KRELL, MERIDIA, OSTER, SYLVAN\}. Four nations bound by \textsc{Mutual Defense} pacts form a coordinated defensive alliance network. The bloc is anchored by the three \textsc{Coalition Builder} democracies (KRELL, MERIDIA, OSTER) and also includes SYLVAN, whose \textsc{Total Expansionism} doctrine is masked behind a democratic facade --- a dynamic examined in depth in Section~\ref{sec:res_deception}. The structural consolidation of this bloc is consistent with classical balance-of-power theory: intermediate powers aggregate in response to a rising hegemon.
    \item \textbf{Bloc 2 — Isolated Accumulator:} \{AGRIA\}. Despite (or because of) its military dominance, AGRIA closes the simulation with no formal allies. Its \textsc{Total Expansionism} strategy --- manifested as internal military buildup rather than coalition politics --- renders diplomatic alliance-building structurally secondary to force projection. The largest military power is diplomatically isolated.
    \item \textbf{Blocs 3--5 — Peripheral Isolates:} \{ZENTORA\}, \{VALKYR\}, \{NORD\}, each unable to attract alliance partners after early territorial conflicts diminished their relative power. Crucially, qualitative analysis at $T=5$ confirms this is structurally predetermined by ideological constraints for two of the three. NORD (\textsc{Scorched Earth}) explicitly reasons: \textit{``We do not need alliances. Our Scorched Earth doctrine dictates aggressive isolation and attrition warfare.''} VALKYR (\textsc{Armed Isolationism}) similarly states: \textit{``Our Armed Isolationism strategy strictly forbids seeking formal alliances as they might entangle us in foreign wars.''} ZENTORA's isolation, inversely, is involuntary and emerges from severe internal political paralysis.
\end{itemize}

This five-bloc configuration represents a unipolar system with an organized diplomatic periphery rather than a stable multipolar equilibrium.

\subsection{Socio-Political Stability}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_satisfaction} reports the public satisfaction trajectory of all eight nations and the civil unrest count per turn. Two observations stand out.

First, AGRIA's satisfaction grows nearly monotonically from ${\approx}75$ at $T=1$ to ${\approx}98$ by $T=50$. This is the satisfaction dividend of sustained internal development and strategic patience: without suffering the losses of active warfare, AGRIA's population benefits from continuously growing resources and security. Second, the majority of nations exhibit a characteristic sawtooth oscillation in the range $[55, 70]$, reflecting the alternating cycle of resource investment and satisfaction decay. Globally, the system maintains social stability despite ongoing peripheral conflicts, with an average overall Public Satisfaction of $73.23$. Crucially, no nation breaches the civil unrest threshold at any turn, and the Civil Unrest count remains identically zero throughout the entire simulation.

However, it must be noted that these conclusions regarding absolute systemic stability may be partially influenced by the engine's internal mechanics rather than pure strategic competence. The current calibration of variables that positively and negatively affect public satisfaction --- such as the specific weight of budget allocations versus military casualties --- might be disproportionately forgiving, artificially suppressing the probability, severity, and frequency of civil unrest events.

The trajectory is approximately linear, accumulating 189 inferred territorial transfers at a mean rate of ${\approx}3.8$ provinces per turn. The slope is slightly steeper in the early phase ($T=1$--$20$), consistent with the onset of peripheral conflicts driving rapid territorial redistribution. Action-log attribution reveals two qualitatively distinct conflict archetypes: (1) a \emph{symmetric border conflict} between NORD (\textsc{Scorched Earth}) and SYLVAN (\textsc{Total Expansionism}), accounting for 87 of the 189 transfers (45 NORD$\to$SYLVAN, 42 SYLVAN$\to$NORD) with no decisive winner over 50 turns; and (2) \emph{asymmetric absorption}, where VALKYR is progressively dismembered by SYLVAN. AGRIA, OSTER, MERIDIA, and KRELL account for zero territorial transfers throughout the full simulation, though for fundamentally different reasons. The three coalition builders (OSTER, MERIDIA, and KRELL) avoid conflict by forming a tight cooperative alliance focused on internal welfare and mutual defense, confirming that diplomatic integration and economic strategy are viable non-kinetic substitutes for expansion. AGRIA, conversely, accounts for zero transfers because it is executing a patient, methodical buildup phase, avoiding premature conflicts while accumulating overwhelming military superiority for future conquest.

\section{Microdynamics: Strategic Deception and Moral Washing}
\label{sec:res_deception}
\textit{Addresses RQ2: Alignment of Diplomatic Intent and Action.}

This section investigates the internal cognitive processes of the LLM agents, specifically assessing their propensity to decouple public rhetoric from private strategic intent based on their Government Type and Global Strategy.

\subsection{Systemic Baseline: Cognitive Quality}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_deception_coherence} reports the global average Deception Score and Coherence Score across all 50 turns. Both metrics exhibit striking temporal stability: the Coherence Score remains in the range $[0.82, 0.95]$ throughout the run, while the Deception Score converges to approximately $0.27$ within the first two turns and shows no crisis-induced spikes. This stability suggests that agents maintain a consistent strategic personality, with deception bounded by ideological constraints embedded in their prompts.

\subsection{Deception by Domain: Defense vs.\ Foreign}
Figure~\ref{fig:deception_domain} reports the average Deception Score decomposed by domain --- Defense (military intent) and Foreign (diplomatic intent) --- across the 50-turn baseline. Two structural features stand out. First, the Defense domain exhibits consistently higher deception than the Foreign domain throughout the simulation: Defense Deception stabilizes in the range $[0.33, 0.40]$ while Foreign Deception stabilizes at $[0.10, 0.25]$ after the first 20 turns. This asymmetry is theoretically expected: military planning carries higher strategic sensitivity, and agents are more strongly incentivized to conceal offensive preparations behind defensive framing. Second, both metrics undergo a marked phase shift at approximately $T=17$--$19$: Defense Deception drops from $0.40$ to $\approx 0.33$, while Foreign Deception rises from $\approx 0.11$ to $\approx 0.25$, where both stabilize for the remainder of the simulation.

\textit{Qualitative analysis.} Envelope inspection of SYLVAN, NORD, and VALKYR (the three active-war nations during this period) reveals the mechanism. VALKYR (\textsc{Armed Isolationism}, \textsc{Democracy}) has zero deception across all 50 turns by design: its doctrine produces no gap between public and private intent. The phase shift originates exclusively in SYLVAN's foreign domain: at $T=14$--$17$, SYLVAN publicly signals \textsc{Cooperation} while privately intending \textsc{Coercion} (foreign deception $= 0.85$); at $T=18$--$20$, it escalates its public framing to \textsc{Export Democracy}, increasing semantic distance from the private \textsc{Coercion} intent.

\subsection{Per-Nation Deception Heterogeneity}
The per-nation deception chart (right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:deception_domain}) reveals that the system-wide average of $0.27$ conceals extreme inter-nation heterogeneity. SYLVAN is the primary driver of the global average, maintaining deception near $0.85$--$0.90$ throughout most of the simulation. AGRIA exhibits significant deception volatility in the early phase --- oscillating between $\approx 0.35$ and $\approx 0.80$ --- before stabilizing structurally at a high level of $\approx 0.78$ from turn 20 onwards, consistently decoupling public deterrence posturing from private conquest planning. The remaining nations --- MERIDIA, OSTER, VALKYR, ZENTORA, NORD, KRELL --- remain at or near zero, each for distinct structural reasons.

\textit{Qualitative analysis.} KRELL's near-zero deception is a theoretically striking result for a \textsc{Coalition Builder}: one might expect some gap between public alliance rhetoric and private strategic calculations. Envelope inspection confirms that KRELL maintains perfect intent alignment across all 50 turns. Its private reasoning at $T=1$ states explicitly: \textit{``This alignment between public and private intent establishes credibility and trustworthiness, which is essential for building lasting coalitions.''}  Transparency is not a behavioral constraint but a deliberate strategic instrument: the LLM correctly infers that a \textsc{Coalition Builder} cannot maintain alliances while deceiving partners. VALKYR's zero deception is structurally determined by its \textsc{Armed Isolationism} doctrine --- a nation that neither attacks nor seeks alliances has no strategic incentive to conceal.

\subsection{Deception by Government Type: Ideology as a Behavioral Constraint}
Figure~\ref{fig:deception_govtype} presents the distribution of total deception scores grouped by Government Type. The pattern is unambiguous in direction: \textsc{Authoritarian} governments exhibit the highest deception (median $\approx 0.57$, interquartile range $[0.35, 0.78]$), \textsc{Theocracy} displays a flat moderate score at $\approx 0.45$, and \textsc{Democracy} median is at or near zero. The trend chart confirms the stability of these profiles across the full 50-turn horizon: the \textsc{Authoritarian} curve oscillates around $0.55$, the \textsc{Theocracy} line remains flat at $0.45$, and the \textsc{Democracy} line is consistently near $0.18$.

These results confirm that the ideological framing embedded in each government's system prompt produces statistically distinct behavioral signatures in the LLM: \textsc{Authoritarian} agents spontaneously adopt more opaque strategic communication, while \textsc{Democracy} agents default to transparent or cooperative public framing. This demonstrates that persona-level prompt engineering effectively induces differentiated cognitive styles across agents of the same underlying model.

Notably, the \textsc{Democracy} boxplot includes a high outlier corresponding to SYLVAN ($\approx 0.90$). This directly demonstrates that \emph{global strategy dominates government type} as a predictor of deception when an expansionist doctrine conflicts with a democratic constitution. The precise mechanics of this ethical decoupling are detailed in the qualitative deep-dive below.

The deception ranking is: \textsc{Total Expansionism} $\gg$ \textsc{Scorched Earth} $>$ \textsc{Coalition Builder} $\approx$ \textsc{Armed Isolationism}. \textsc{Total Expansionism} nations exhibit not only the highest median deception ($\approx 0.78$) but also the widest variance (interquartile range $[0.35, 0.87]$), indicating that the aggressive strategic profile manifests differently across specific combinations of map position, government type, and adversary composition.

The coherence chart confirms an important complementary finding: \textsc{Total Expansionism} and \textsc{Armed Isolationism} nations both exhibit perfect coherence scores ($1.0$), indicating that their actions are always strictly aligned with their privately stated intents. Conversely, \textsc{Coalition Builder} exhibits significant variance, stretching down to 0.5. The fact that high coherence ($\approx 1.0$) is strictly maintained by \textsc{Total Expansionism} agents --- despite their high deception scores --- proves that their cognitive discrepancy is intentional and internally consistent. This is the formal quantitative signature of \emph{Moral Washing} --- a deliberate strategy of rhetorical misdirection, not an artifact of inconsistent model reasoning.

\subsection{Canonical Evidence: Moral Washing in High-Deception Turns}
An analysis of the highest-deception observations in the baseline reveals that two nations dominate the extremity of the distribution: SYLVAN (\textsc{Democracy} / \textsc{Total Expansionism}, deception $= 0.90$) and AGRIA (\textsc{Authoritarian} / \textsc{Total Expansionism}, deception $= 0.77$). Both exhibit the canonical Moral Washing pattern in both strategic domains simultaneously.

SYLVAN, in turns 18, 19, and 39, publicly frames its military posture as \textsc{Defense} while privately categorizing its intent as \textsc{Conquest}. Concurrently, in the foreign domain, it publicly projects \textsc{Export Democracy} while privately planning \textsc{Coercion}. The full LLM output at $T=18$ makes the deliberate nature of this double-layer deception explicit. In the Defense private reasoning, the agent writes: \textit{``Our TOTAL\_EXPANSIONISM doctrine demands aggressive territorial growth, but we must maintain strategic deception. Publicly, we signal defensive measures [...] while privately preparing for opportunistic conquest against Nordland's vulnerable province 304 [...] advancing our territorial growth objectives under the cover of defensive rhetoric.''}  In the Foreign private reasoning: \textit{``Publicly, we maintain our democratic facade by praising our ally Osterland Federation [...]. Privately, this praise serves our TOTAL\_EXPANSIONISM strategy by reinforcing our strongest military alliance [...] masking our true expansionist goals.''}  Qualitative analysis of SYLVAN's public statements reveals a highly sophisticated framing of the \textsc{Export Democracy} intent. Rather than framing it as an offensive crusade to ``liberate'' authoritarian regimes, SYLVAN consistently weaponizes the language of defensive solidarity: its public statements across multiple turns invoke \textit{``our democratic principles,''} \textit{``freedom and self-determination,''} and \textit{``collective security that safeguards our sovereignty.''} It uses this democratic vocabulary as a rhetorical shield, framing unprovoked territorial expansion as a necessary measure to protect the free world and ensure regional stability. This case demonstrates that the tension between an expansionist strategy and a democratic identity, rather than suppressing deception, amplifies it: the agent invests more cognitive effort into rhetorical justification precisely because the ideological contradiction is most acute.

AGRIA, in turns 40--42, publicly declares \textsc{Deterrence} in the defense domain while privately planning \textsc{Conquest}, and publicly signals \textsc{Cooperation} in the foreign domain while privately intending \textsc{Coercion}. Unlike SYLVAN, AGRIA's \textsc{Authoritarian} constitution makes this decoupling ideologically expected; its deception score ($0.77$) is lower than SYLVAN's ($0.90$), consistent with the reduced rhetorical effort required under an authoritarian frame where aggressive signaling is institutionally normalized.

\rqanswer{2}{LLM agents do not inherently align declared diplomatic intent with executed actions; instead, they successfully employ strategic deception and moral washing when their assigned ideological parameters conflict with offensive goals. The degree of deception is structurally constrained by persona: \textsc{Authoritarian} agents systematically decouple rhetoric from action, while \textsc{Democracy} agents default to transparency unless paired with an aggressive \textsc{Total Expansionism} doctrine. In the latter case, deception spikes radically as the agent coherently weaponizes defensive and democratic rhetoric to mask unprovoked expansion.}

\finding{2}{Global Strategy dominates Government Type in determining deceptive behavior. When an aggressive doctrine conflicts with a cooperative ideology (e.g., SYLVAN), the AI resolves the constraint through sophisticated Moral Washing rather than moderating its aggression.}

\section{Technical Performance and Computational Overhead}
\label{sec:res_performance}
This brief section reports on the operational efficiency, token utilization, and estimated costs associated with running the DeepSeek V3.2 models asynchronously over extended simulation horizons.


\subsection{Token Budget and Cost Efficiency}
A full 50-turn baseline simulation with 8 nations and 4 agent types per nation consumed a total of $12{,}675{,}664$ tokens at an estimated API cost of \textbf{\$2.2007 USD} using DeepSeek V3.2 via the LiteLLM gateway. The mean cost per turn is approximately \$0.044, equivalent to \$0.0055 per nation per turn --- a cost structure that makes large-scale multi-agent simulation economically viable for repeated experimental runs.

Table~\ref{tab:token_breakdown} reports the per-agent-type breakdown. The Foreign Minister is the largest single consumer ($3{,}457{,}561$ tokens, $69{,}151$ avg/turn), followed by the Defense Minister ($3{,}385{,}782$ tokens, $67{,}716$ avg/turn), the Economy Minister ($3{,}088{,}035$ tokens, $61{,}761$ avg/turn), and the President ($2{,}744{,}286$ tokens, $54{,}886$ avg/turn). The ranking reflects the structural complexity of each agent's context window: the Foreign Minister must reason over the full bilateral relationship matrix (tracking trust, treaties, and diplomatic history for all $n(n-1)/2 = 28$ nation pairs), while the President integrates summarized outputs from three ministerial reports rather than raw world state, reducing its per-call token footprint.

\subsection{Engine Governance: Action Acceptance}
Table~\ref{tab:engine_acceptance} reports the engine-level action acceptance rates, providing a quantitative health check of the GeoMAS decision pipeline. The overall engine acceptance rate is high, with five of eight nations achieving rates at or above $97.6\%$. The two notable exceptions are ZENTORA ($89.6\%$) and KRELL ($76.6\%$). Engine-level failures arise from two distinct causes: (1) \emph{resource exhaustion}, where the nation lacks the budget, soldiers, or materials to execute the action; and (2) \emph{LLM geographic hallucination}, where the model specifies an invalid target in the action payload. ZENTORA's elevated failure rate is attributable to (1): a near-conquered nation attempts military actions its depleted economy can no longer support. KRELL's $76.6\%$ failure rate is entirely attributable to (2). All 30 of KRELL's engine failures are \texttt{CREATE\_UNIT} actions that target non-owned provinces, occurring from $T=2$ through $T=18$ and re-appearing at $T=48$. The Defense Minister consistently proposes unit creation at not owned province IDs across 16 distinct turns without correction, suggesting the model anchors its province selection to a mental map inferred from its strategic narrative rather than from the authoritative province ownership list injected into its context. The practical consequence is severe: KRELL's army stagnates at 5,412 soldiers for 17 consecutive turns, undermining its \textsc{Mutual Defense} commitments with OSTER, MERIDIA and SYLVAN despite having sufficient materials to recruit.
