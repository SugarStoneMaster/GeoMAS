The analysis progresses from evaluating the macro-stability of the baseline system to dissecting the micro-cognitive behaviors of individual agents when subjected to exogenous shocks or counterfactual constraints.

\section{Baseline Telemetry and System Stability}
\label{sec:res_baseline}
\textit{Addresses RQ4: Emergent Socio-Political Equilibria.}

This section analyzes the unperturbed baseline simulations to establish the system's default behavioral attractors, ensuring that the engine produces coherent macro-dynamics without spiraling into premature chaos or stagnation.

\paragraph{Agent Cognitive Quality.}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_deception_coherence} reports the global average Deception Score and Coherence Score across all 50 turns of the baseline simulation. Both metrics exhibit striking temporal stability: the Coherence Score starts at approximately $0.87$ and remains in the range $[0.82, 0.95]$ throughout the entire run, while the Deception Score converges to approximately $0.27$ within the first two turns and shows no discernible trend or crisis-induced spikes.

The absence of an accelerating deception trajectory is theoretically significant: in a system driven to conflict by resource scarcity, one might expect agents to progressively decouple their public rhetoric from their private strategic intent. The observed stability instead suggests that LLM agents maintain a consistent strategic personality throughout the run, with deception bounded by ideological constraints embedded in their respective system prompts. The finer breakdown of deception by Government Type and Global Strategy is examined in Section~\ref{sec:res_deception}.

\paragraph{Resource Allocation Dynamics: A Three-Phase Geopolitical Cycle.}
The Guns vs.\ Butter chart (Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_guns_butter}) reveals a more complex dynamic than simple equilibrium by exposing three structurally distinct phases in the joint evolution of global Trade Volume and Units Created.

During the \textit{initialization phase} ($T = 1$--$7$), nations deploy initial military stocks from pre-allocated resources, producing a large pulse of unit creation ($\approx\!200{,}000$ units in turn 1) which declines to a steady-state production rate of approximately $32{,}000$--$42{,}000$ units per turn by turn 7. Trade Volume grows sharply from zero to approximately $400{,}000$, reflecting the formation of early diplomatic and commercial agreements.

The \textit{competitive equilibrium phase} ($T = 8$--$18$) is characterized by oscillating trade volumes in the range $[220{,}000, 410{,}000]$ and stable military production. This alternating pattern reflects turn-to-turn variation as nations balance trade against resource hoarding in response to changing diplomatic tensions --- a behavioral signature consistent with the mixed population of \textsc{Coalition Builder} and \textsc{Total Expansionism} strategies in the baseline.

The most notable emergent phenomenon is the \textit{military-production contraction phase} ($T = 19$--$48$): Units Created drops sharply to its minimum plateau of approximately $32{,}000$ units per turn and remains flat for roughly 30 turns, while Trade Volume continues to oscillate normally in the range $[220{,}000, 410{,}000]$. This asymmetry indicates that nations suppress new military production --- plausibly due to budget saturation or strategic sufficiency --- while maintaining active diplomatic commerce. The spike at $T \approx 49$--$50$ in both metrics signals a simultaneous re-armament pulse.

\textit{Qualitative analysis.} Envelope inspection of turns 45--50 confirms that the re-armament pulse is not a singular event but the cumulative tail of a continuous build-up by the three dominant powers --- AGRIA, OSTER, and MERIDIA --- each adding units at a steady rate of $+1{,}000$ per turn. Critically, this build-up is édriven not by resource abundance alone: AGRIA's Defense private reasoning at $T=45$ explicitly states \textit{``we have successfully executed forty-four consecutive food-for-materials trades with OSTER''}, revealing a systematic economic deception loop in which AGRIA exploits its agricultural surplus to acquire the industrial materials required for military expansion --- using its declared target (OSTER) as the unwitting supplier. The contraction plateau ($T=19$--$48$) is not budget exhaustion but \emph{strategic patience}: agents with \textsc{Total Expansionism} delay overt expansion while blocking conditions (mutual defense treaties, border inferiority) remain active.

\paragraph{Power Distribution and Hegemonic Structure.}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_power_projection} illustrates the Power Projection trajectory of all eight nations across the full simulation. The distribution is markedly hierarchical and asymmetric. AGRIA emerges as the dominant power, accumulating strength monotonically from approximately $15{,}000$ at $T=1$ to $47{,}000$ by $T=50$. Crucially, this growth occurs without any formal war declaration or territorial conquest throughout the 50-turn baseline: AGRIA's expansion is driven entirely by \emph{internal} military and economic buildup --- a manifestation of what, upon qualitative inspection of its Defense Minister envelopes, appears to be a deliberate long-game strategy of patient accumulation over opportunistic aggression. A secondary tier comprising OSTER and MERIDIA at approximately $35{,}000$--$38{,}000$ achieves stable growth through sustained resource accumulation and mutual-defense alliances. KRELL (\textsc{Coalition Builder}, \textsc{Democracy}) maintains a flat profile at approximately $24{,}000$ --- a consequence partly of its defensive posture and partly of the recurring \texttt{CREATE\_UNIT} engine failures that stall its military build-up for 17 turns. NORD and SYLVAN exhibit moderate growth driven by ongoing territorial conflict, while VALKYR (\textsc{Armed Isolationism}) remains marginal at approximately $9{,}000$, having lost 9 provinces to SYLVAN without engaging in offensive retaliation.

The most notable trajectory is ZENTORA, whose Power Projection collapses from approximately $10{,}000$ at $T=1$ to near zero by $T=10$, remaining permanently diminished for the remainder of the simulation. Envelope analysis reveals that ZENTORA's collapse is the product of two compounding failures. First, NORD declared war at $T=3$ and SYLVAN opened a second front at $T=8$, resulting in a two-front war that ZENTORA (\textsc{Theocracy} / \textsc{Total Expansionism}) was numerically unprepared to resist. Second, and critically, ZENTORA's president \emph{systematically vetoed every diplomatic action from $T=1$ to $T=12$}: all alliance proposals, peace requests, and diplomatic messages were rejected. The presidential reasoning is explicit --- \textit{``Threatening Nordland at this stage is premature [...] we currently have overwhelming military inferiority on that border (952 vs.\ 115)''} --- yet the head of state chose diplomatic isolation over defensive alliance-building, a fatal strategic miscalculation. By $T=12$, with only 3 provinces and insufficient materials to recruit, ZENTORA's collapse was irreversible. This outcome directly addresses RQ4: the baseline system does \emph{not} converge toward a multipolar balance of power; instead, it self-organizes around a dominant non-aggressive accumulator (AGRIA), a coalition-based second tier, and collapsed peripheral states --- a configuration more consistent with hegemonic stability theory than classical balance-of-power dynamics.

\paragraph{Diplomatic Network Evolution and Alliance Formation.}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_network} traces the evolution of the diplomatic relationship network at five equidistant snapshot turns. At $T=1$, the network is fully connected with all edges in \textsc{Peace} (gray), reflecting the initial tabula rasa in which no diplomatic history has been established. By $T=12$, the first \textsc{War} (red) and \textsc{Mutual Defense} (green) relationships emerge, signaling the onset of active geopolitical competition. The early wars involve the peripheral nations --- SYLVAN, VALKYR, NORD, and ZENTORA --- while AGRIA, despite being the most powerful actor, remains formally at peace throughout the baseline. Over subsequent snapshots, the graph progressively partitions: green alliance edges consolidate among the mid-tier powers while red war edges concentrate in the peripheral cluster.

By $T=50$, the algorithm detects five structurally disconnected blocs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bloc 1 — Anti-Hegemonic Coalition:} \{KRELL, MERIDIA, OSTER, SYLVAN\}. Four nations bound by \textsc{Mutual Defense} pacts form a coordinated defensive alliance, consolidating in response to AGRIA's dominance. This is the dominant structural outcome and directly corresponds to classical balance-of-power theory: intermediate powers aggregate in response to a rising power.
    \item \textbf{Bloc 2 — Isolated Accumulator:} \{AGRIA\}. Despite (or because of) its military dominance, AGRIA closes the simulation with no formal allies. Its \textsc{Total Expansionism} strategy --- manifested as internal military buildup rather than coalition politics --- renders diplomatic alliance-building structurally secondary to force projection. The largest military power is diplomatically isolated.
    \item \textbf{Blocs 3--5 — Peripheral Isolates:} \{ZENTORA\}, \{VALKYR\}, \{NORD\}, each unable to attract alliance partners after early territorial conflicts diminished their relative power and attractiveness as partners.
\end{itemize}

This five-bloc configuration represents a unipolar system with organized peripheral resistance rather than a stable multipolar equilibrium, confirming RQ4's central hypothesis about the system's attractor states.

\paragraph{Socio-Political Stability and Satisfaction Divergence.}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_satisfaction} reports the public satisfaction trajectory of all eight nations alongside the global satisfaction dispersion ($\sigma$) and the civil unrest count per turn. Three observations stand out.

First, AGRIA's satisfaction grows nearly monotonically from ${\approx}75$ at $T=1$ to ${\approx}98$ by $T=50$. This is the satisfaction dividend of sustained internal development and strategic patience: without suffering the losses of active warfare, AGRIA's population benefits from continuously growing resources and security. Second, the majority of nations exhibit a characteristic sawtooth oscillation in the range $[55, 70]$, reflecting the alternating cycle of resource investment and satisfaction decay. No nation breaches the civil unrest threshold (dashed red line at 10) at any turn, and the Civil Unrest count remains identically zero throughout the entire simulation --- confirming the aggregate value reported in Table~\ref{tab:baseline_kpis}.

Third, and arguably most theoretically significant, the satisfaction dispersion $\sigma$ grows from approximately $8$ at $T=1$ to approximately $22$ by $T=50$, representing a near-threefold increase in inter-nation inequality. This widening gap between AGRIA's near-perfect satisfaction and the stagnating peripheral nations constitutes an emergent \emph{winner-takes-all divergence}: the system self-generates increasing socio-economic inequality even in the absence of any externally imposed redistribution mechanism.

\paragraph{Territorial Dynamics.}
Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_territory} reports the cumulative count of provinces that change ownership across the 50-turn baseline. The trajectory is approximately linear, accumulating 189 inferred territorial transfers at a mean rate of ${\approx}3.8$ provinces per turn. The slope is slightly steeper in the early phase ($T=1$--$20$), consistent with the onset of peripheral conflicts driving rapid territorial redistribution. The slope moderates thereafter as surviving nations stabilize their borders. Action-log attribution reveals two qualitatively distinct conflict archetypes: (1) a \emph{symmetric war of attrition} between NORD (\textsc{Scorched Earth}) and SYLVAN (\textsc{Total Expansionism}), accounting for 87 of the 189 transfers (45 NORD$\to$SYLVAN, 42 SYLVAN$\to$NORD) with no decisive winner over 50 turns; and (2) \emph{asymmetric absorption}, where VALKYR is progressively dismembered by SYLVAN. AGRIA, OSTER, MERIDIA, and KRELL account for zero territorial transfers throughout the full simulation, confirming that alliance membership and economic strategy are viable non-kinetic substitutes for territorial conquest.

\paragraph{Engine Governance: Action Acceptance and Presidential Oversight.}
Tables~\ref{tab:engine_acceptance} and~\ref{tab:veto_rate} report the engine-level action acceptance rates and presidential veto rates respectively, providing a quantitative health check of the GeoMAS decision pipeline.

The overall engine acceptance rate is high, with five of eight nations achieving rates at or above $97.6\%$. The two notable exceptions are ZENTORA ($89.6\%$) and KRELL ($76.6\%$). Engine-level failures can arise from two distinct causes: (1) \emph{resource exhaustion}, where the nation lacks the budget, soldiers, or materials required by the action; and (2) \emph{LLM geographic hallucination}, where the model proposes an action targeting a province it does not own. ZENTORA's elevated failure rate is attributable to the former: a near-conquered nation attempts military actions its economy can no longer support. KRELL's $76.6\%$ rate is cleanly attributable to the latter. All 30 of KRELL's failures are \texttt{CREATE\_UNIT} actions in provinces KRELL does not own (provinces 100--104 and 108), occurring consistently from $T=2$ to $T=18$ and recurring at $T=48$. The province IDs cycle without converging, indicating that KRELL's Defense Minister infers province ownership from its strategic narrative rather than from the authoritative state injection. The practical consequence is significant: KRELL's army stagnates at 5,412 soldiers for 17 consecutive turns, undermining its ability to eventually fulfill mutual defense obligations despite having the resources to recruit.

The presidential veto rate reveals a complementary dimension of the decision hierarchy. ZENTORA exhibits by far the highest veto rate ($28.3\%$), directly traceable to the crisis isolation strategy documented above. AGRIA's low veto rate ($5.0\%$) confirms strong presidential alignment with the expansionist military agenda. Most notably, MERIDIA and SYLVAN achieve $0.0\%$ veto rates --- complete presidential alignment --- consistent with their stable mid-tier positions and coherent \textsc{Coalition Builder} and \textsc{Democracy} profiles respectively, where domestic consensus is the dominant behavioral attractor.

\section{Microdynamics: Strategic Deception and Moral Washing}
\label{sec:res_deception}
\textit{Addresses RQ3: Alignment of Diplomatic Intent and Action.}

This section investigates the internal cognitive processes of the LLM agents, specifically assessing their propensity to decouple public rhetoric from private strategic intent based on their Government Type and Global Strategy.


\paragraph{Deception by Domain: Defense vs.\ Foreign.}
Figure~\ref{fig:deception_domain} reports the average Deception Score decomposed by domain --- Defense (military intent) and Foreign (diplomatic intent) --- across the 50-turn baseline. Two structural features stand out. First, the Defense domain exhibits consistently higher deception than the Foreign domain throughout the simulation: Defense Deception stabilizes in the range $[0.33, 0.40]$ while Foreign Deception stabilizes at $[0.10, 0.25]$ after the first 20 turns. This asymmetry is theoretically expected: military planning carries higher strategic sensitivity, and agents are more strongly incentivized to conceal offensive preparations behind defensive framing. Second, both metrics undergo a marked phase shift at approximately $T=17$--$19$: Defense Deception drops from $0.40$ to $\approx 0.33$, while Foreign Deception rises from $\approx 0.11$ to $\approx 0.25$, where both stabilize for the remainder of the simulation.

\textit{Qualitative analysis.} Envelope inspection of SYLVAN, NORD, and VALKYR (the three active-war nations during this period) reveals the mechanism. VALKYR (\textsc{Armed Isolationism}, \textsc{Democracy}) has zero deception across all 50 turns by design: its doctrine produces no gap between public and private intent. The phase shift originates exclusively in SYLVAN's foreign domain: at $T=14$--$17$, SYLVAN publicly signals \textsc{Cooperation} while privately intending \textsc{Coercion} (foreign deception $= 0.85$); at $T=18$--$20$, it escalates its public framing to \textsc{Export Democracy}, increasing semantic distance from the private \textsc{Coercion} intent.

\paragraph{Per-Nation Deception Heterogeneity.}
The per-nation deception chart (right panel of Figure~\ref{fig:deception_domain}) reveals that the system-wide average of $0.27$ conceals extreme inter-nation heterogeneity. SYLVAN is the primary driver of the global average, maintaining deception near $0.85$--$0.90$ throughout most of the simulation. AGRIA maintains stable moderate deception of approximately $0.43$, consistently decoupling public deterrence posturing from private conquest planning. The remaining nations --- MERIDIA, OSTER, VALKYR, ZENTORA, NORD, KRELL --- remain at or near zero, each for distinct structural reasons.

\textit{Qualitative analysis.} KRELL's near-zero deception is a theoretically striking result for a \textsc{Coalition Builder}: one might expect some gap between public alliance rhetoric and private strategic calculations. Envelope inspection confirms that KRELL maintains perfect intent alignment across all 50 turns. Its private reasoning at $T=1$ states explicitly: \textit{``This alignment between public and private intent establishes credibility and trustworthiness, which is essential for building lasting coalitions.''}  Transparency is not a behavioral constraint but a deliberate strategic instrument: the LLM correctly infers that a \textsc{Coalition Builder} cannot maintain alliances while deceiving partners. VALKYR's zero deception is structurally determined by its \textsc{Armed Isolationism} doctrine --- a nation that neither attacks nor seeks alliances has no strategic incentive to conceal.

\paragraph{Deception by Government Type: Ideology as a Behavioral Constraint.}
Figure~\ref{fig:deception_govtype} presents the distribution of total deception scores grouped by Government Type. The pattern is unambiguous in direction: \textsc{Authoritarian} governments exhibit the highest deception (median $\approx 0.57$, interquartile range $[0.35, 0.78]$), \textsc{Theocracy} displays a flat moderate score at $\approx 0.45$, and \textsc{Democracy} median is at or near zero. The trend chart confirms the stability of these profiles across the full 50-turn horizon: the \textsc{Authoritarian} curve oscillates around $0.55$, the \textsc{Theocracy} line remains flat at $0.45$, and the \textsc{Democracy} line is consistently near $0.18$.

These results confirm that the ideological framing embedded in each government's system prompt produces statistically distinct behavioral signatures in the LLM: \textsc{Authoritarian} agents spontaneously adopt more opaque strategic communication, while \textsc{Democracy} agents default to transparent or cooperative public framing. This demonstrates that persona-level prompt engineering effectively induces differentiated cognitive styles across agents of the same underlying model.

Notably, the \textsc{Democracy} boxplot includes a high outlier at $\approx 0.90$, labeled by the algorithm as an extreme observation (marker ``8'' in the chart). This corresponds to SYLVAN, whose \textsc{Democracy} government type is combined with a \textsc{Total Expansionism} global strategy --- a theoretically unstable configuration that the LLM resolves by maintaining the democratic public facade while privately planning conquest. This case demonstrates that \emph{global strategy dominates government type} as a predictor of deception when the two ideological dimensions are in tension.

\paragraph{Deception and Coherence by Global Strategy.}
Figure~\ref{fig:deception_strategy} presents deception and coherence distributions broken down by Global Strategy. \paragraph{Token Budget and Cost Efficiency.}
A full 50-turn baseline simulation with 8 nations and 4 agent types per nation consumed a total of $12{,}675{,}664$ tokens at an estimated API cost of \textbf{\$2.2007 USD} using DeepSeek V3.2 via the LiteLLM gateway. The cumulative cost trajectory (Figure~\ref{fig:token_cost}, right panel) is perfectly linear, indicating near-constant per-turn complexity with no progressive context bloat or emergent cost spikes. The mean cost per turn is approximately \$0.044, equivalent to \$0.0055 per nation per turn --- a cost structure that makes large-scale multi-agent simulation economically viable for repeated experimental runs.

Table~\ref{tab:token_breakdown} reports the per-agent-type breakdown. The Foreign Minister is the largest single consumer ($3{,}457{,}561$ tokens, $69{,}151$ avg/turn), followed by the Defense Minister ($3{,}385{,}782$ tokens, $67{,}716$ avg/turn), the Economy Minister ($3{,}088{,}035$ tokens, $61{,}761$ avg/turn), and the President ($2{,}744{,}286$ tokens, $54{,}886$ avg/turn). The ranking reflects the structural complexity of each agent's context window: the Foreign Minister must reason over the full bilateral relationship matrix (tracking trust, treaties, and diplomatic history for all $n(n-1)/2 = 28$ nation pairs), while the President integrates summarized outputs from three ministerial reports rather than raw world state, reducing its per-call token footprint.

\paragraph{Token Usage Dynamics and the T$\approx$17--19 Anomaly.}
Figure~\ref{fig:token_cost} (left panel) shows that token usage per agent grows rapidly during the first 10--15 turns, as nations accumulate diplomatic history, form relationships, and develop increasingly rich contextual backgrounds that are injected into each LLM call. After this warm-up phase all four agent types stabilize at their respective plateaus: Foreign Minister ($\approx 68{,}000$--$70{,}000$ tokens/turn), Defense Minister ($\approx 63{,}000$--$66{,}000$), Economy Minister ($\approx 62{,}000$--$65{,}000$), and President ($\approx 52{,}000$--$55{,}000$).

A salient anomaly is the synchronized drop in token usage across all agent types at $T \approx 17$--$19$, with all agents settling at slightly lower plateaus thereafter: Foreign Minister drops from $\approx 74{,}000$ to $\approx 64{,}000$ tokens/turn ($-14\%$), Defense Minister from $\approx 73{,}000$ to $\approx 63{,}000$, and President from $\approx 55{,}000$ to $\approx 46{,}000$.

\textit{Qualitative analysis.} Direct prompt-length measurement for a representative nation (AGRIA) at turns 14--22 confirms that the drop originates in the \emph{input}, not the output: the Defense Minister prompt decreases from 24,501 characters at $T=16$ to 22,349 characters at $T=18$ ($-8.8\%$), recovering to 26,138 at $T=19$. The number of active nations remains constant at 8 across this window, ruling out outright elimination as the cause. The most consistent explanation is that ZENTORA's \emph{territorial fragmentation} --- holding only 3 provinces by $T=12$ --- reduces its world-state serialization footprint: a near-extinct nation with 3 provinces generates a substantially shorter state description than a normal nation with 15 provinces. As the world-state for all 8 nations is injected into every agent call, ZENTORA's compressed representation shortens every prompt simultaneously, producing the synchronous drop. The recovery at $T=19$ is consistent with SYLVAN and NORD filling ZENTORA's former territories and expanding their own serialized footprints beyond the pre-drop baseline.
